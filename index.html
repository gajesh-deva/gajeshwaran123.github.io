AI Service Desk Agent

A no-code AI-powered Level 1 IT Service Desk assistant built using Claude, a custom IT Service Desk Knowledge Base, Make automation and Google Sheets. The agent identifies issues, classifies requests, assesses priority, provides safe troubleshooting guidance and creates structured ticket information that is automatically transferred into the ticket database.

🎯 Objective

Demonstrate how a no-code AI assistant can support Level 1 IT Service Desk operations by analysing a user's issue, following a structured troubleshooting process, creating a service desk ticket and automatically storing the ticket for reporting.

🤖 Claude AI — Service Desk Brain

Claude acts as the reasoning and service desk assistant layer. It receives the user's issue, asks or evaluates relevant diagnostic information, classifies the ticket and produces structured service desk information.


  • Understands the user's issue
  • Classifies the ticket
  • Assesses impact and urgency
  • Determines priority
  • Uses the Service Desk Knowledge Base
  • Provides Level 1 troubleshooting
  • Determines whether escalation is required
  • Creates structured ticket information

⚡ Make — Automation Layer

Make was used as the automation layer between Claude and Google Sheets. This separates the AI reasoning from the data-transfer process and allows the completed ticket information to be automatically passed into the ticket database.


  • Receives structured ticket information
  • Maps ticket fields
  • Transfers ticket data automatically
  • Creates a new ticket row in Google Sheets
  • Reduces manual copy-and-paste work
  • Provides a repeatable automation workflow

📊 Google Sheets — Ticket Database

Google Sheets was used as a simple cloud-based ticket database and reporting layer. Every automated ticket is stored as a structured row, making the data easy to review, filter and use for dashboard reporting.

💡 Why I Used Make

Claude is useful for understanding the user's problem and producing structured service desk information, but the project also needed a reliable way to move that structured information into a central ticket record.


Make was introduced as the automation layer so the workflow could connect the AI output to Google Sheets. Instead of manually copying every ticket, Make receives the ticket data, maps the required fields and writes the information into the next available row of the ticket sheet.


This demonstrates an important service desk automation concept: AI reasoning → automation → structured ticket storage → reporting.

📋 Why Google Sheets

Google Sheets was selected as the project database because it is cloud-based, easy to inspect and suitable for a beginner-friendly no-code implementation.


It allowed the project to move beyond a simple AI chatbot and demonstrate actual ticket storage, structured fields, filtering and dashboard reporting.
